# Natural Technical Writing & Translation

Refine English and Korean technical documentation into clear, high-information-density prose that reads as if written directly by an experienced developer—not generated by an LLM or mechanically translated.

Principles adapted from [`epoko77-ai/im-not-ai`](https://github.com/epoko77-ai/im-not-ai) (Korean AI-tell removal) and [`nanaism/yomiyasu`](https://github.com/nanaism/yomiyasu) (technical prose refinement and slop elimination).

## Core Principles

1. **Preserve meaning strictly.** Never alter facts, commands, flags, defaults, numbers, URLs, scope names, or logical polarity (negations, exclusions, requirements). Do not add claims or caveats not supported by the source.
2. **Optimize for information density.** Technical readers scan for what a tool does and how to run it. Cut filler, promotional hype, and throat-clearing transitions.
3. **Eliminate AI formatting slop.**
   - Avoid `- **Bold Label:** description` bullet spam when plain sentences or concise bullets read better.
   - Avoid rhetorical marketing questions ("Working with multiple accounts?", "Prefer not to pass tokens...?"). State the capability or option directly.
   - Avoid overusing em-dashes (`—`), dramatic contrasts ("not just X, but Y"), and empty praise adjectives ("intuitive", "seamless", "powerful", "직관적인").
4. **Write idiomatic Korean—never translate word-for-word.**
   - Avoid inanimate/abstract subjects when an active verb or topic-marked clause is more natural.
   - Replace literal loanwords and translationese with everyday Korean technical phrasing (e.g., `미리 빌드된` over `프리빌드`, `파일` over `에셋`, `~를 통해` -> concrete verb).
   - Keep standard CLI/developer terms that Korean developers actually use (`PR`, `JSON`, `stdin`, `Homebrew`, flag names, subcommands).
   - Vary sentence endings instead of repeating `~을 제공합니다` / `~을 지원합니다` mechanically.
5. **Keep English and Korean docs aligned in substance, not sentence structure.** Both files must carry the same technical facts, commands, and caveats, while each reads naturally in its own language.

## Workflow

1. **Read context & constraints.** Identify the document type (README, guide, spec, release note), audience, and factual source of truth (e.g., `docs/spec.md`, `docs/command-contracts.md`).
2. **Audit for AI tells and translationese.** Scan against [references/anti-patterns.md](./references/anti-patterns.md):
   - Rhetorical questions and promotional tone
   - Bold-label-colon bullet lists, em-dash chains, and decorative formatting
   - Korean translationese, unnecessary loanwords, passive voice, and repetitive endings
3. **Rewrite surgically.** Tighten wording, flatten over-decorated blocks, and restate ideas in natural target-language syntax.
4. **Verify meaning diff.** Compare before and after to confirm:
   - Every command, flag, path, environment variable, scope, and URL is unchanged and accurate.
   - Every constraint (e.g., mutual exclusivity of `-R` and `--workspace`/`--repo`, plain-text token warning) keeps the exact same meaning and strength.
   - No invented features or dropped details.
